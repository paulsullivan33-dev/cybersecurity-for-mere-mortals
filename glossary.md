# Glossary

One plain sentence for every jargon term used in this guide.

- **Threat model** — Your answer to "who might come after me, and
  what would it cost me?" It decides which defenses are worth
  your time.
- **Attack surface** — Everything an attacker could reach: every
  open port, every account, every installed app. Smaller is
  safer.
- **2FA** (two-factor authentication) — Logging in with two
  different kinds of proof (something you know *plus* something
  you have), so a stolen password alone isn't enough.
- **Passkey** — A login credential stored on your device that
  proves who you are with a fingerprint or PIN — phishing can't
  steal it because there's nothing to type into a fake page.
- **Phishing** — Fake messages (email) designed to trick you into
  handing over passwords or clicking something malicious.
- **Smishing** — Phishing by text message.
- **Vishing** — Phishing by voice call.
- **Credential stuffing** — Taking passwords leaked from one site
  and trying them on many others, betting that you reused them.
- **Malware** — Any software that does harm: viruses, trojans,
  spyware, all of it.
- **Ransomware** — Malware that locks your files and demands
  payment for the key.
- **Firewall** — A gatekeeper that blocks network traffic unless
  a rule allows it.
- **VPN** (virtual private network) — An encrypted tunnel from
  your device to another network. It hides your traffic from
  the local network — it does *not* make you anonymous or
  immune to scams.
- **SSH** (Secure Shell) — The standard way to get a command
  line on a remote machine, encrypted.
- **Port forwarding** — A router rule that sends traffic arriving
  on a port from the internet to a specific machine inside your
  network.
- **Honeypot** — A fake, tempting machine that exists to watch
  what attackers do.
- **fail2ban** — A tool that temporarily blocks IP addresses
  after too many failed login attempts.
- **UPnP** (Universal Plug and Play) — A router feature that
  lets devices open port-forwarding rules by themselves, with
  no password and no asking you.
- **WPA2 / WPA3** — The security standards for Wi-Fi passwords.
  WPA3 is newer and better; WPA2 is still fine with a strong
  password.
- **Backup 3-2-1** — Keep 3 copies of important data, on 2
  different kinds of storage, with 1 copy offsite.
- **Social engineering** — Manipulating a person into handing
  over access, instead of breaking the technology. Most scams
  are social engineering.
- **Zero-day** — A bug attackers know about before the software
  maker does, so there's no fix yet. You can't defend against a
  specific zero-day — you defend by updating fast once the fix
  ships.
